About

Short Bio

I am a PhD student in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. My research sits at the intersection of machine learning and power electronics reliability: I build learning systems that stay trustworthy when the physics shifts — across unseen operating conditions, multi-physics couplings, and time scales a thousand× apart.

My current work develops self-supervised time-series foundation models for power-converter health monitoring that generalize to unseen conditions (PE-TSFM, IEEE TIE), and load-robust multi-physics attention models for joint electrical–thermal estimation (PE-ETT, under review). Earlier, I worked on physically interpretable, hardware-accelerable neural-dynamics thermal models for PMSM drives (IEEE TVT / TEC) and physics-informed neural networks for remaining-useful-life prognostics (Adv. Eng. Informatics). Experiments run on a 5 kW three-phase traction-inverter platform with real-time RT-Box control and 100 kHz acquisition, accelerated power-cycling aging, and non-invasive electrical/thermal sensing.

Before PolyU, I received my M.Eng. in Information and Communication Engineering from Northwestern Polytechnical University and my B.Eng. in Computer Science and Technology from Ocean University of China — a path from computing into power electronics that still shapes how I approach reliability problems.

Research

Research Interests

Foundation Models for Power Electronics

Self-supervised time-series transformers pre-trained on converter telemetry, evaluated under leave-one-condition-out protocols for health monitoring that survives unseen loads, devices, and missions.

Physics-Informed Machine Learning

Embedding heat-transfer and degradation dynamics into network structure — from self-attention-assisted PINNs for RUL prognostics to neural ODEs with physical interpretability.

Thermal Modeling of Drives & Modules

Lightweight neural-dynamics models for PMSM and IGBT-module temperature estimation, designed to be parallelizable and hardware-accelerable (FPGA-friendly) for embedded deployment.

Prognostics & Health Management

Non-invasive monitoring of bond-wire, solder-layer, DC-link capacitor, and TIM degradation; multi-timescale electrical (100 kHz) × thermal (100 Hz) fusion for reliability assessment.
